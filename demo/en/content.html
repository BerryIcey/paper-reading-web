Figure 1 · Read a panel, understand the comparison

What does this comparison show?

Group A has a higher observed output than group B. That difference alone does not establish its cause.

Question and design

Compare two groups on the same measurement scale. Each bar represents one group summary, not an individual participant.

Read the actual observation

The orange bar is group A (60); the blue bar is group B (35). Begin with those labels before interpreting the bar heights.

What follows—and what remains unknown?

The plotted summaries differ. Without repeated observations or uncertainty estimates, we cannot tell whether the difference is stable. A follow-up needs replication and an appropriate control.

Why this matters in the argument

This comparison motivates the next question: what explains the difference? The next method must distinguish the plausible explanations rather than merely repeat the observation.

Know what the evidence does not establish

Different bar heights are an observation. A causal explanation requires a design that distinguishes alternatives. These numbers are invented for interface demonstration.

Look up a concept without losing your place

Control: what is the reference for this comparison?

A control supplies a reference for interpreting a measurement. In this demo, A and B share a measurement scale. In a real paper, you must also check whether background differences, treatments, and sampling support the proposed comparison.

Plotted unit: what does each point or bar represent?

Identify whether an element represents a sample, gene, genomic region, or summary before interpreting a pattern.
